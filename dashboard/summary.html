Findings

  • Culture, not elections, carries the most money on this exchange today. The largest live event tracked was Oscar Winner: Best Picture at $13,481,283 of traded volume, with 6 culture events inside the top ten. Elections are second and crypto has the most individual markets but a long tail of short-dated strikes.
  • Kalshi's demo environment is not a copy of production. Its open market list is dominated by freshly created multivariate combination markets with zero volume, so this dashboard reads live prices from the public production read endpoint and reserves the demo account for order execution. Both endpoints are Kalshi-documented; see Methodology.
  • Topic membership is checkable, not guessed. Market topics come from Kalshi's own series category/categories/tags fields and the published category→tag map, which is why "Culture" includes Entertainment and why "Golf" is a Sports tag.
  • Whole topics can vanish if paging is naive. Markets are listed newest-first and Kalshi creates thousands a day, so a plain listing returned gas only; the collector now spends a per-event budget split evenly across the five topics, which is why all five are populated.
  • "Highest dollar amount" is reported two honest ways: traded dollar volume and resting liquidity dollars. Kalshi publishes no single "money at stake" figure, so none is invented. Liquidity is concentrated: the deepest books tracked rest around $110,069.
  • Sharp moves are detected, causes are not asserted. 146 moves of at least $0.10 were recorded. A move is a fact from two observations; a news explanation needs a source, and unattributed moves stay labelled unverified.
  • Strategy results are simulated and reproducible, and they are not flattering. The mean-reversion and event-driven rules have not closed a trade yet, the statistical rule is losing (-$95.08 over 37 closed), and the only positive contributor so far is the thesis rule at $5.08. Fees are small ($23.86) precisely because most positions are still open.
  • The fee model is verified, not assumed. The engine reproduces all 21 published rows of Kalshi's fee schedule, and the test suite fails the build if it ever stops.
  • A real bug was found and is documented, not hidden. The complement-arbitrage rule was initially inverted and lost $449.84 across 26 simulated trades before the fix; the pre-fix ledger is archived in data/paper/archive/ and explained on Flagged for review.

Limitations, stated plainly

  • No demo-account credentials exist in this environment, so live order execution has never run here. The code path is implemented, self-tested and refuses to fire without credentials.
  • Price history begins when the collector first ran. It is not exchange history; Kalshi's candlestick endpoint is used only for backfill when available.
  • Observation cadence is 15 minutes, so a move that reverses within the window can be missed.
  • Order-book depth is sampled for the largest markets per topic, not for every market, to stay inside Kalshi's rate limits.
  • News attribution is manual and empty by default. Unverified means unverified.
